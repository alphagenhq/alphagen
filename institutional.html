A client asks. Your advisor is ready.

Three real examples from recent AlphaGen briefings. Every number comes straight from the company's own release or SEC filing.

Equities
Client"Is the big tech story still holding up?"
Advisor, from the latest briefing"The latest reports were strong. Apple posted September-quarter record revenue of $114.5 billion, up 12% from a year ago, with a September-quarter record for iPhone and an all-time high in Services. Palantir's revenue rose 96% to $2.31 billion, and for the first time its U.S. commercial revenue, $984 million, was larger than its U.S. government revenue."
Sources: Apple Q4 FY2026 results release · Palantir Q3 2026 results (SEC Form 8-K), Nov. 2, 2026
Capital markets
Client"Are companies still doing big deals?"
Advisor, from the latest briefing"Yes. On October 27 alone, Guidewire agreed to buy hyperexponential for $1.7 billion in cash plus up to $400 million tied to revenue targets, funded partly with a new $1.0 billion term loan. Ares Capital agreed to acquire MidCap Financial Investment Corporation in a deal valued at about $2.6 billion in total enterprise value, paid mostly in Ares stock."
Sources: Guidewire press release · Ares Capital press release (SEC Form 8-K, Ex. 99.1)
Earnings
Client"Is it only tech that's doing well?"
Advisor, from the latest briefing"No. Chevron earned $5.06 per diluted share in the third quarter, up from $1.82 a year earlier. Regeneron's revenue rose 36% to $5.1 billion, and Canadian National Railway's revenue grew to C$4.77 billion from C$4.17 billion. Energy, healthcare, and industrial companies all reported growth."
Sources: Chevron, Regeneron, and CN Q3 2026 results releases (SEC filings), Oct. 30, 2026

Questions firms ask

Built to fit how your firm works

Which markets does it cover?

Equity markets come first: movers, sectors, and earnings. Capital markets follow: IPOs, M&A, buybacks, and debt issuance. Every edition then adds the Fed, Treasury yields, credit, macro data, and global events that drive them.

Is this investment advice?

No. Briefings are general market education and commentary. They contain no recommendations, price targets, or personalized advice, so advisors can share the ideas within your firm's existing policies.

Can our compliance team review it?

Yes. Briefings arrive on a fixed schedule in a consistent format, and every figure links to its primary source, which makes review fast.

Can it carry our brand?

Yes. Private-label editions use your firm's name, logo, colors, and disclosures. Your advisors and clients see your brand, not ours.

Where does the information come from?

Primary sources only: company earnings releases and SEC filings, the Federal Reserve, U.S. Treasury, Bureau of Labor Statistics, Bureau of Economic Analysis, and Census Bureau.

How is pricing set?

By plan and by the number of advisors at your firm. Request a pilot and we'll send pricing with your sample week.
